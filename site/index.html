About this tracker

Frontswatch shows which force has held each of Yemen’s 22 governorates at 15 points between September 2014 and October 2026, plus dated events: strikes and attacks, ground fighting, changes of control, political decisions and humanitarian news. Choose a day, then choose how far back to look.

How to read it

  • Colours show the controlling force: Houthis, the internationally recognised government (the Presidential Leadership Council since April 2022), the Southern Transitional Council (STC), and in 2015 and 2016 al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula (AQAP).
  • Stripes mean two forces hold different districts inside one governorate. They do not mark a front line.
  • Every event carries a status: confirmed, reported, claimed or reference. The Sources tab explains each one. Claims by a party to the conflict are never shown as confirmed.
  • The Outlook tab is an editorial reading of the reporting, not a statistical forecast.

Limits

Control is simplified to the governorate level. Marker positions are approximate for places the sources describe loosely. Events from 2014 to 2021 rest on general reference sources and are less precise than 2022 to 2026. Battlefield claims change quickly. This is an independent project, not affiliated with any government, party, armed group or news organisation.

Methodology

Each event comes from published reporting: official Saudi and coalition statements carried by the Saudi Press Agency, news outlets (Al Jazeera, Al Arabiya, Asharq and Anadolu), research and monitoring groups (ACLED, the Long War Journal, and ISW and Critical Threats posts), Security Council Report, and Wikipedia pages used as an index to the underlying reporting. Every event links to its sources so you can check it.

Control of each governorate is taken from the same reporting and reviewed at 15 points in time. A governorate is shown as mixed when two forces hold different parts of it. A whole district is shaded only when a source names it.

Events are added by hand, not by an automatic feed. Wording follows the sources and avoids taking sides. Where sources disagree, the event says so, and claims by a party to the conflict stay labelled as claims until someone independent confirms them.

Attacks on Saudi Arabia: missile, drone and other attacks on Saudi territory or Saudi vessels are listed only when a Saudi official body (the coalition spokesman, the civil aviation authority, a ministry or civil defence, usually via the Saudi Press Agency) has announced them, and they are described as that body describes them. Reports of such attacks that Saudi authorities have not announced are not included.

The Arabic text is a translation of the English and may contain errors. Source titles stay in their original language.
